Guide / Our rules

How we talk about meaning

Many people choose a stone for what it stands for. We respect that, and we want to talk about it honestly: where each association comes from, who makes it, and what it does not promise.

These associations come from traditions and beliefs; they have no proven effect. No stone heals or guarantees any outcome.

Try it in the Atelier

What we do

  • We report traditions and beliefs, saying which ones and how many sources mention them.
  • Every suggestion states the rule that produced it. You can change everything afterwards in the Atelier.
  • Glass and ceramic remain colours: we give them no meaning and never present them as stones.
  • The new stones are virtual for now: indicative images, prices to be confirmed, treatments to be checked with our suppliers.

The Four Pillars (bazi)

The Four Pillars (八字, bāzì) are a traditional Chinese way of writing a date and time of birth with eight calendar characters: a heavenly stem and an earthly branch for the year, month, day and hour. We compute them with the open-source library lunar-javascript (MIT licence), in your browser.

Rule (a simplified method, one school among others): we count the elements of the eight characters (the month branch counts twice); if those supporting the day element make up at least half, the day element is called “strong” and we keep the element it generates and the one it controls; otherwise (day element called “weak”), the element that generates it and the day element itself. Other schools may conclude differently; take it only as colour inspiration.

  • We do not ask for the place of birth and do not compute true solar time: the date and hour are used as given.
  • The day changes at 11 pm: between 11 pm and midnight, enter the next day and choose 子. Without an hour, there is no hour pillar.
  • The count uses only the main element of each branch (no hidden stems).
  • In this method, the year pillar changes at Lichun (立春, around 4 February); the animal of the “birth year”, however, follows Chinese New Year. The two can therefore differ for a birth between those dates.

The result gives only colours and stones: no character traits, no forecast.

A sentence

Rule: the words recognised in your sentence give an intention and colours; then the same rule as for intentions, and the colour picks the supporting stone.

By default, your sentence is read in your browser: we look for words (intentions, colours) in the three languages. It is neither sent nor stored.

An AI mode, off by default, may be turned on later. It only sorts the sentence into intentions and colours and never writes text: explanations always come from our tables. If it is on, your sentence is sent to our provider (OpenRouter, which passes it to the Mistral Small 3.2 model) only after you click “Refine with AI”, and ORIMEA does not keep it.

Where sources disagree

  • Zodiac signs: published lists barely overlap. That is why we give our own rule (colours by element) and, alongside, a list common in France.
  • Chinese zodiac animals: “one animal, some stones” tables almost never agree; we follow one published table based on the cycle of elements.
  • Birthstones: the American and British lists differ for several months; we say which one we use.
  • Sign dates: they vary by one day depending on the source.
  • The same stone can change meaning from one culture to another: amethyst points to study in China and to calm in France.

Why we do not talk about health

Many sites attribute effects on the body or on mood to stones. None has been demonstrated, and French law strictly regulates any health claim. So we repeat no such statement and offer no health-related intention. For any medical question, please see a health professional.

These associations come from traditions and beliefs; they have no proven effect. No stone heals or guarantees any outcome.

Your data stays on your device

Intention, date, hour and sentence are processed in your browser. Nothing is sent, nothing is kept. A saved composition keeps only the beads, never your date or your sentence. The only exception is the AI mode described above, and only after you click.
