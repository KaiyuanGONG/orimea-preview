Glass, crystal, rock crystal: three words, three materials

Rock crystal is quartz; the “crystal” of decanters is a glass defined by an EU directive; glass is glass. What the rules say, and how we name our beads.

Updated September 30, 2026 · 3 min readUnder review

In brief

  • Rock crystal is colourless quartz, a natural mineral.
  • The “crystal” of tableware is a glass rich in lead oxide or other oxides, defined by an EU directive from 1969.
  • For jewellery, the French consumer authority has found the word “cristal” used for glass. At ORIMEA, glass is called glass.

The word “crystal” covers very different materials: a natural stone, a kind of glassware and sometimes, wrongly, any transparent bead. Here is how to tell them apart.

Rock crystal is quartz

According to the International Colored Gemstone Association (ICA), colourless quartz is called rock crystal (also sold as “clear quartz”); the word comes from the Greek krustallos, “ice crystal”. The GIA notes that it was carved into balls and bowls in ancient times, and that amethyst is the purple variety of the same mineral.

So rock crystal is a natural mineral, in the same family as amethyst, citrine and smoky quartz.

Glass is not a crystal

In a crystal, atoms are arranged in a regular pattern. Glass is amorphous: its atoms have no such order. The International Gem Society puts it in passing: “crystal” is an inappropriate adjective for glass.

“Crystal”: a defined word for glass

Yet the word has a precise meaning for some glass. A 1969 EU directive on crystal glass (69/493/EEC) reserves four names for glass of a defined composition. Annex I gives the names in each EU language (in English: full lead crystal, lead crystal, crystal glass); the table below uses the French ones. According to the official copy of Annex I:

Name Minimum content Minimum density
cristal supérieur (full lead crystal) 30% lead oxide 3.00
cristal au plomb (lead crystal) 24% lead oxide 2.90
cristallin 10% oxides of zinc, barium, lead or potassium, singly or together 2.45
verre sonore 10% oxides of barium, lead or potassium, singly or together 2.40

The directive also sets a refractive index and, for verre sonore, a hardness. It applies to products under heading 70.13 of the Common Customs Tariff: we have not checked whether that covers beads. It does show that “crystal” in the legal sense is a glass, never rock crystal.

What the rules say for jewellery

In France, the DGCCRF (the French consumer protection authority) requires “the exact name of the gemstone or pearl” on the label and on any commercial or advertising document, including for online sales. The terms “semi-precious” and “semi-fine” are banned, and “precious” cannot describe an imitation.

In a survey of costume jewellery published in 2017, it noted among other things the name “cristal” being used for glass.

Decree no. 2002-65, as reproduced by a gemmology expert, also provides for the qualifier “imitation” (« d’imitation ») for artificial products that imitate the look of a stone.

In Chinese: 水晶, 玻璃 and 琉璃

In Chinese, 水晶 is the name for rock crystal (the mineral being quartz, 石英), according to the table of names in China’s national standard GB/T 16552 (2010 edition); we write 白水晶 (“white crystal”) to be clear. The standard puts the character 仿 (“imitation”) before the name of the imitated stone, for example “仿水晶（玻璃）” (imitation rock crystal, made of glass). For artificial stones, it bans misleading names. A 2017 edition has been in force since 2018 (SAMR); we could not consult its text.

Glass also has old names: 琉璃, 颇梨 or 药玉, as the People’s Daily recalls; under the Ming and Qing it was called 料器, according to the Palace Museum. When our Chinese pages say 琉璃 for a lampwork bead, that is the meaning: glass.

“Opalite”: a trade name

“Opalite” says nothing about the material. According to the International Gem Society and Geology.com, the name mostly refers to an opalescent glass that imitates opal or moonstone. A natural opal also goes by that name, and in 1989 the GIA’s journal reported it for a plastic imitation opal.

At ORIMEA

  • “Clear quartz” (rock crystal, cristal de roche, 白水晶) always means natural quartz: see the clear quartz material sheet.
  • Our glass beads are called glass: clear glass, opaline glass, lampwork glass bead. Their sheets say so: “It is glass”, and opaline glass is not opal.
  • We never call glass “crystal”, and we do not use the word “opalite”.
  • We choose glass and ceramic beads for their colour only: we give them no meaning.
