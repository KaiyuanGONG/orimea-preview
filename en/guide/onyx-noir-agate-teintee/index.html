Black onyx: usually dyed chalcedony

The even black of “onyx” beads almost always comes from a dye. What onyx means in the strict sense, how it is dyed, and how we name it.

Updated September 30, 2026 · 2 min readUnder review

In brief

  • Onyx, agate and jasper are varieties of chalcedony: quartz made of crystals too small to see with the naked eye.
  • Natural black chalcedony is rare; according to the GIA, virtually all black chalcedony on the market is treated.
  • We keep the trade name “black onyx” and state on the material sheet that the stone is usually dyed.

In the trade, “black onyx” means a bead of deep, even black. The name is convenient, but it hides two things: the stone is not really onyx, and its black almost always comes from a dye.

What onyx is

The GIA lists onyx, together with agate and jasper, as varieties of chalcedony, a form of quartz made of crystals too small to be seen with the naked eye.

Strictly speaking, onyx is chalcedony with straight, parallel black and white bands. A stone that is black all over should therefore be called “black chalcedony”, as Gary Roskin pointed out in the trade magazine JCK in 2002 – while noting that the whole industry says “black onyx” anyway.

Almost always dyed

The GIA is clear: what many call “black onyx” is actually dyed black chalcedony, and since natural black chalcedony is rare, virtually all black chalcedony is treated. Agate lends itself to this: it is porous and takes dyes well.

JCK describes two methods:

  • an organic dye, which may fade in sunlight;
  • a more durable process: the stone soaks in a sugar bath, then goes into hot sulphuric acid, which carbonises the sugar in its pores.

The idea is not new. In the GIA journal Gems & Gemology (1984), Kurt Nassau quotes a recipe from Pliny the Elder: lumps of stone, probably agate, boiled in honey for seven days and nights. In his view, adding a final heating step would give a process close to the modern sugar-and-acid method.

What the rules say

In France, the DGCCRF (the French consumer protection authority) states that the treatments a stone has undergone – irradiation, laser, dye and so on – must be disclosed to the consumer. Dyeing is one of the examples it names; for such treatments, the seller must provide an information sheet describing the treatment, its effects and the precautions to take.

In China, in the national table classifying treatments (as reproduced by a trade website), dyeing agate is classed as an “enhancement” (优化) rather than a “treatment” (处理). We use the plainest wording: the stone is very probably dyed.

Polished or matte

We offer two finishes, polished and matte. The material is the same, and the question of the dye is the same too.

At ORIMEA

  • Our black onyx beads are virtual for now: indicative images.
  • The polished black onyx and matte black onyx sheets say: “Usually an agate (chalcedony) dyed black and sold under the trade name ‘onyx’. Treatment to be confirmed with the supplier.”
  • If the dye is organic, do not leave your bracelets in full sun for long; when you are not wearing them, keep them out of direct light.
