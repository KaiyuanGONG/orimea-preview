Is citrine heated?

Usually, yes: most citrine on the market is heated amethyst. What that means, what the rules say, and how we state it.

Updated September 30, 2026 · 2 min readUnder review

In brief

  • Citrine is a yellow to orange quartz; its colour comes from a trace of iron.
  • It is rare in nature: most citrine sold is amethyst that has been heated.
  • Its material sheet says so – most citrine on the market is heated amethyst – until a supplier gives us a written declaration.

  2. Can you tell with the naked eye?

  4. What about amethyst?

Citrine is the yellow to brownish-orange variety of quartz: a variety of the same mineral as clear quartz, amethyst and smoky quartz. According to the GIA (Gemological Institute of America), its colour comes from a trace of iron in the crystal.

Rare in nature

The GIA puts it plainly: citrine is rare in nature, and “most citrine on the market is the result of heat treatment of amethyst”. China’s national gem testing centre (NGTC) says the same: citrine is “mostly obtained by heating amethyst”.

In practice, amethyst is heated – sometimes whole geodes, the GIA notes – and the purple turns yellow or orange. The result is still quartz: not glass and not an imitation, but natural quartz that has been heated.

Can you tell with the naked eye?

We know of no reliable way to tell with the naked eye on a bead, and we will not invent one. Without a lab test, we assume the most common case: heated amethyst.

What the rules say

In France, the trade in gems is governed by decree no. 2002-65, which the government decided in 2025 to keep unchanged. The DGCCRF (the French consumer protection authority) explains that when stones have undergone a “traditional lapidary practice” – using colourless fluids and/or heating – the consumer must be told by a clearly legible notice. Heating is therefore something to disclose, and our material sheet does.

In China the vocabulary differs. In the national table classifying treatments (as reproduced by a trade website), heating quartz is classed as an “enhancement” (优化) rather than a “treatment” (处理). The same practice is not labelled the same way everywhere, so we use the plainest wording: the stone is probably heated.

What about amethyst?

Amethyst is the starting material, but it is also sold in its own right. The NGTC notes that its colour is not stable: it can fade with strong heat or long exposure to sunlight. When you are not wearing your bracelets, keep them out of direct light.

At ORIMEA

  • The citrine material sheet says: “Most citrine on the market is heated amethyst. Heating to be confirmed with the supplier.”
